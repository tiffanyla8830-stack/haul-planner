Six short sections — open the one you need. The first is open by default.

The app starts empty. No example contracts or routes ship with it, so the lists, the Overview and every total begin at zero until you add something. For a first contract, scan a screenshot or press + New contract.
Using the planner
1. Scan or type a contract.
On the Contracts tab, drop a screenshot into the reader (or paste with Ctrl/Cmd+V) and hit Scan screenshot. The text it reads lands in the box underneath — check it against the image, fix anything wrong, then press Apply to contract. Nothing enters the contract until you do.

2. Build the stops.
Each stop takes a type, a location and its SCU. Drag the ≡ handle to reorder them, or use the ▲▼ buttons. Order matters — it sets the travel-time chain. Copy duplicates a stop in place, which is quicker than retyping a shared pickup. The Notes field above the stops is free text for anything worth remembering later.

3. Check the plan.
The haul plan shows total SCU, trip count, hold percentage, time and payout. Anything that does not add up is listed in red — fix those before you fly. If you set a max container size on the contract, the plan warns you when the chosen ship cannot load those boxes.

4. Save and finish it.
Everything saves as you type, so there is nothing to lose by closing the tab. Use the arrow on a contract in the left-hand list and choose Complete. It moves to the completed group and the Overview totals update with it.
Personal hauls
A normal contract pays you a flat fee for moving someone else’s cargo. A personal haul is cargo you buy and sell yourself — so the profit is what you sold for, minus what you paid.

Tick Personal haul in Contract details and the payout field gives way to a costs panel: buy cost per SCU, a live total spend, and a sale value field.

Complete a personal haul and you are asked what the load sold for. Enter it there, or leave it blank and fill it in later from the same costs panel. Profit is green when you made money, red when the sale came in under the spend.

Bought, sold and profit totals appear on the Overview, with a per-haul table. Until you record a sale, a haul counts as a loss — you have spent the money and own cargo worth something unrecorded.
Saved routes
Routes are trades you repeat — a commodity, what you pay per SCU, what you sell it for, and where. Enter the buy and sell costs yourself from the in-game kiosk; the planner does not guess market prices. The margin and profit per trip work themselves out from what you enter.

Click the ☆ on a route to favourite it. Use Send to contracts to turn a route into a haul job, or Save as route on a contract to go the other way once you have built a haul worth repeating.

Run counts. Every time you send a route to contracts the app tallies it — how many times you have run it, when you last did, and what it has paid. The count shows on the route in the list, so a favourite you fly weekly looks different from one you starred once and forgot.

The list sorts by name, margin, buy cost, sell cost, favourites, times run or last run, and archived routes can be hidden.
Sorting and finding things
Both lists carry a toolbar: pick a sort key, use the ↕ button to reverse the order, and tick the checkbox to show or hide the parked ones — completed contracts, archived routes.

Contracts sort by most recent, name, SCU, payout or stop count.
Routes sort by most recent, name, margin per SCU, buy cost, sell cost, favourites first, times run, or last run.

Notes. Each contract has a free-text Notes field above its stops, for anything worth remembering — a server that misbehaved, a route to avoid, who to fly with. A contract carrying a note shows a small ✎ marker in the list.

The Overview gathers it up: completed contracts, personal hauls, a per-commodity breakdown, favourite routes with their run counts, SCU totals and the buy/sell profit tables.
Sharing this file
This is one self-contained file. Send it however you like — email, Discord, a drive, a USB stick. The other person double-clicks it and it opens in their browser. No install, no account, no server.

Your data does not travel with it. Contracts and routes are stored in whichever browser opens the file, so each person sees only their own. To move your data to another machine, use Export to file and have them Import from file.

Keep each copy somewhere fixed. Two copies in two folders keep two separate sets of data, because the browser ties storage to the file’s location.

Sending the file does not send your work. Whoever receives it opens an empty app, exactly as you first did — which is why Export to file is the way to pass a set of contracts on.
Good to know
The scanner reads multi-stop contracts written like Deliver 0/125 SCU of Titanium to Everus Harbor with Collect Titanium from CRU-L1 Ambitious Dream Station, and simpler Pickup: / Dropoff: forms too. On a multi-leg contract the quantities are summed, so three legs of 125 / 105 / 133 read as 363 SCU.

A full-resolution screenshot works far better than a photo of the screen. If a scan comes back garbled, the raw read is sitting in the text box — correct it there and hit Apply. That route needs nothing external.

First scan online. The first time anyone scans a screenshot, the file loads an OCR engine from a public CDN. Everything else runs inside the browser, and no data is sent anywhere.

Prices. Route economics use the buy and sell costs you type. Ship cargo capacities and container sizes are real figures; treat any other number as your own input rather than market data.

Where the money came from. The Overview's By commodity table answers “which good has made me the most”. A contract carries several goods on one payout, so its profit is split across its cargo lines in proportion to SCU. If the quantities are left blank it splits evenly, and the totals always reconcile to the contract figure — nothing is invented.

Import safety. Choosing to replace your data on import downloads a copy of what you had first, named tifft0e-before-import-….json. Merging never overwrites anything, so it takes no snapshot.

Reset. At the foot of the Overview, Reset everything clears every contract and route. Your data lives only in this browser, so there is no undo — export a backup first if you might want any of it back.
